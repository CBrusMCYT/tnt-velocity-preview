Guide (How to use)


This page explains every button, field and setting of the TNT Simulation Chamber, one by one.








What it does


The chamber simulates primed TNT in Minecraft Bedrock Edition (approximated). You place TNT, give it a fuse and a starting velocity, and watch how each explosion pushes the other TNT. Everything starts paused, so you can set things up first and press Play when ready.





Menu (three-line button, top left)


    

  • TNT Simulation Chamber: goes to the main screen (the simulator). This is the default page.
    • 

  • Guide (How to use): opens this page.
    • 

  • Theme: changes the look of the whole site. See Themes below.
    • 

  • × (or tapping outside the menu, or pressing Esc): closes the menu.
    • 






Themes


    

  • Light (Default): the standard light theme.
    • 

  • Dark: a dark theme.
    • 

  • Light Bordered (Dark Border): the light theme with thick black borders and squarer corners, similar to the Blockbench look.
    • 

  • Dark Bordered (White Border): the dark theme with thick white borders and squarer corners.
    • 

  • OreUI (Minecraft style): a square, beveled look modeled on the dark Ore UI menus of Minecraft Bedrock: charcoal panels, light grey buttons with a darker bottom edge, green primary buttons and dark text fields. It uses the Minecraft Ten font if it is installed or placed at fonts/minecraft-ten.ttf (Mojangles at fonts/Mojangles.ttf is the second choice), and falls back to a normal font otherwise. It is an approximation, not an official Mojang theme.
    • 

  • Custom: your own theme. Choosing it opens an editor:

      

    • Theme name: the name stored in the exported file.
      • 

    • Main color: page background. Panel color: panels, inputs and the menu.
      • 

    • Text color and Muted text color: normal text and secondary text.
      • 

    • Button color and Button text color: ordinary buttons.
      • 

    • Accent color and Accent text color: the main buttons (Play, Spawn TNT) and selected items.
      • 

    • Canvas color: the background of the viewport. Grid color: grid lines and numbers. Ground color: the floor in the Side view.
      • 

    • Use border color (optional): when off, borders use a soft color derived from your colors. When on, you can pick the Border color and the Border width (0 to 4 px).
      • 

    • Corner radius: how round panels and buttons are (0 to 24 px).
      • 

    • Export .tntheme: saves the custom theme as a file (the text also appears in the box below). Import .tntheme: loads a theme file. Import from text: loads theme text pasted into the box. Reset custom theme: restores the default custom colors.
      • 

    • 



Your theme choice and custom colors are remembered in this browser.





Top bar


    

  • Side / Top (only in 2D): Side shows X to the right and Y (height) up. Top looks down from above, with X to the right and Z downward.
    • 

  • 2D / 3D: switches between the flat view and the 3D view. Each view keeps its own camera position and zoom.
    • 






Viewport


Info box (top left): shows REC while recording, PAUSE while paused, the current Tick, the number of TNT alive, the highest TNT speed (max v, in blocks per second) and a note that arrows are velocity multiplied by 6.


Drawing: each TNT is a block (a square in 2D, a textured cube in 3D). Blue arrows show velocity, orange lines are trails of where TNT have been, and a fading circle marks each explosion's push radius (8 blocks for the Bedrock formula, 4 for Custom). Grid numbers show block coordinates. In the Side view and in 3D the floor is at Y = 0.


Camera:


    

  • 2D: drag to pan. Mouse wheel or a two-finger pinch zooms.
    • 

  • 3D: drag to rotate (or pan when Drag: Pan is on). Two fingers pan and pinch-zoom. On a computer, Shift + drag or right/middle-button drag pans, and the wheel zooms.
    • 






Control buttons (under the viewport)


    

  • Play / Pause: runs or pauses the simulation (20 ticks per second at 1x speed). The button shows the action it will do next.
    • 

  • Record / Stop: records the viewport as a 1024 x 1024 video at 60 FPS. It needs at least one TNT. The scene stays frozen for a moment, then plays, and recording stops by itself when every TNT has exploded and the blast circles have faded (or after 3000 ticks, or when you press Stop). The video is saved when it ends, and a preview with a "Save video" button appears under the buttons. The format is mp4 when the browser can make it, otherwise webm.
    • 

  • +1 tick: advances the simulation by exactly one tick.
    • 

  • Reset: removes all TNT and trails, clears the log and the saved start states. Stops a running recording.
    • 

  • Focus: moves and zooms the camera to frame all TNT and their trails.
    • 

  • Center Camera: moves the camera center to 0, 0, 0 without changing the zoom.
    • 

  • Drag: Rotate / Drag: Pan: in 3D, chooses what a one-finger or mouse drag does. In 2D, dragging always pans.
    • 

  • Save current state to .mcstructure: saves every TNT you spawned since the last Reset, in the state it had when spawned, as a Bedrock structure file. It works before or after pressing Play. See File formats.
    • 






Settings panel


    

  • Speed: 0.1x, 0.25x, 1x or 3x of normal speed.
    • 

  • Knockback: Bedrock uses the Bedrock formula (see Physics rules). Custom (4 b/s, 4 blocks) uses a simple linear push with a maximum of 4 blocks per second and a range of 4 blocks.
    • 

  • Explosion order: what happens when several TNT explode in the same tick. One after another processes them in spawn order, so each explosion pushes TNT that have not exploded yet and they explode at different positions. All at once lets every TNT whose fuse ends in that tick explode together, using the positions they have at that moment.
    • 

  • Recording background: Theme, White, Black, Green or Transparent for recorded videos. Transparent hides the grid and floor and needs a browser that can record webm with transparency.
    • 

  • Arrows: shows or hides velocity arrows. Trails: shows or hides trails. Floor: turns the Y = 0 floor on or off (without it TNT fall forever). TNT border: draws an outline around each TNT.
    • 



These settings are remembered in this browser.





Spawn TNT panel


    

  • At: where the TNT is placed (its feet position). X, Y in the Side view; X, Y, Z in the Top view and in 3D (Y is the height).
    • 

  • Tick: the fuse. Normal (80) is 80 ticks (4 seconds). Int lets you type your own number of ticks; 0 makes the TNT explode the moment it is spawned.
    • 

  • Velocity: the starting speed of the TNT along X, Y and Z (Z is hidden in the Side view). The dropdown sets the unit: B/t blocks per tick, B/s per second, B/m per minute, B/h per hour (B = block, t = tick, s = second, m = minute, h = hour).
    • 

  • Spawn Momentum: false adds nothing. true adds the push a freshly lit TNT gets: 0.2 blocks per tick upward and 0.02 blocks per tick sideways in a random direction (only along X in the Side view). It is added on top of Velocity.
    • 

  • Count: how many TNT to spawn (1 to 500). The dropdown sets the layout: Stack puts all of them at the same point; Cube arranges them in a grid with 1 block spacing, centered on X and Z with the first layer at Y.
    • 

  • Spawn TNT: spawns the TNT into the scene using the values above.
    • 

  • + Preset: adds the values above to the Draft list in the Presets panel instead of spawning.
    • 

  • Example: resets the scene and spawns a TNT with a normal fuse next to a TNT that explodes instantly, then frames them.
    • 






Presets panel


    

  • Draft: the list of entries you added with + Preset. Remove deletes one entry.
    • 

  • Preset name and Save Preset: saves the draft as a preset and clears the draft. Saving with an existing name replaces that preset.
    • 

  • Saved presets: each card shows the name and the total number of TNT, with these buttons:

      

    • Play: resets the scene, spawns the preset and starts the simulation.
      • 

    • Record: the same, but also records a video.
      • 

    • More opens a menu: Load spawns the preset and leaves it paused. Save to .mcstructure writes the preset straight to a Bedrock structure file without loading it. Edit puts the preset back into the draft so you can change it (save with the same name to replace it). Export .tntpreset saves just this preset as a file. Delete removes it (tap twice to confirm).
      • 

    • 

  • Export all (.tntpreset): saves every preset in one file. Import (.tntpreset): loads presets from a file. The text box shows exported text and also accepts pasted text; Import from text loads what is in the box.
    • 



Presets are remembered in this browser.





File formats


.tntpreset is a JSON text file. Each preset has a tnt list; every entry describes one Spawn TNT setup:


{
  "format": "tntpreset",
  "version": 1,
  "presets": [
    {
      "name": "Example",
      "tnt": [
        {
          "position": [2, 0.5, 0],
          "amount": 16,
          "layout": "stack",
          "momentum": false,
          "fuse": 1,
          "velocity": { "value": [10, 0, 10], "type": "bps" }
        }
      ]
    }
  ]
}


    

  • position: X, Y, Z. amount: integer from 1 to 500. layout: "stack" or "cube". momentum: true or false. fuse: ticks (0 = instant).
    • 

  • velocity.value: X, Y, Z. velocity.type: "bpt" (blocks per tick), "bps" (per second), "bpm" (per minute) or "bph" (per hour).
    • 



.tntheme is also JSON. It holds the custom theme: name, colors (main, panel, text, mutedText, button, buttonText, accent, accentText, canvas, grid, ground), border (enabled, color, width) and radius.


.mcstructure is a Bedrock structure file with one primed TNT per TNT, holding its position, starting velocity (Velocity plus the momentum rolled when spawned) and fuse. Fuse is limited to 127 in the file. Positions are shifted if any coordinate is negative, and the log says so. TNT with fuse 0 explode as soon as the structure is loaded, and the game works out the knockback itself. Large structures take longer to load in the game.

Physics rules


    

  • Each tick: gravity pulls TNT down by 0.04 blocks per tick, air drag multiplies velocity by 0.98, and the fuse counts down by 1. When the fuse reaches 0 the TNT explodes.
    • 

  • Floor: on the ground, horizontal speed is multiplied by 0.4 every tick and the TNT does not bounce.
    • 

  • Explosion (Bedrock knockback): power 4, centered 0.06125 above the exploding TNT. Another TNT gets a push of 1 - (distance - 0.49) / 8 blocks per tick, where distance is measured to its feet. Beyond about 8.49 blocks there is no push. The push is added to its velocity and follows the explosion sphere: it points from the explosion center to the TNT's feet. The exception is a TNT whose origin (X, Y, Z) is exactly the same as the exploding TNT's: it is pushed straight up. Even a tiny offset switches it back to the normal sphere direction.
    • 

  • Custom knockback: (1 - distance / 4) times 0.2 blocks per tick, so at most 4 blocks per second, and nothing beyond 4 blocks.
    • 

  • Exposure is always 1, because there are no blocks to shield the blast.
    • 

  • These values come from community data and the Minecraft Wiki and are not verified against the game. Compare with Bedrock before relying on exact numbers.
